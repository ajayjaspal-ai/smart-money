📊 Credit Score Guide

Understand CIBIL score, credit report, common mistakes and practical ways to build a healthier credit profile.

What is a Credit Score?

A CIBIL Score is a 3-digit summary of your credit history. It ranges from 300 to 900. Lenders use the score along with other information when evaluating loan or credit-card applications.

Remember: A credit score is important, but it is not the only factor in a lender's decision.

Understanding the 300–900 Range

ScoreHow to think about it
300–599Lower score range; review repayment history and outstanding credit carefully.
600–699Room for improvement; focus on consistent on-time payments and controlled balances.
700+CIBIL states that a score above 700 is generally considered good.
750+A commonly referenced strong range; still, lenders use their own eligibility criteria.

These bands are a practical guide, not a universal lender approval rule.

What Affects Your CIBIL Score?

1. Payment History

Late or missed EMI/card payments can negatively affect your score. Paying dues on time is one of the most important habits.

2. Credit Utilization

Using a high portion of your available credit can indicate higher credit burden. Keep balances under control.

3. Age of Credit

The length of time you have maintained credit accounts contributes to your credit profile.

4. New Enquiries

Frequent applications for new loans or cards can create multiple lender enquiries and may affect your score.

6 Practical Rules to Improve Your Score

  1. Pay every EMI and credit-card bill on time.
  2. Keep credit-card balances under control instead of repeatedly using most of the available limit.
  3. Avoid applying for many loans/cards within a short period.
  4. Keep track of active and closed accounts in your credit report.
  5. Check your report for errors such as incorrect overdue amounts, account status or enquiries.
  6. Give good credit behaviour time to build. There is no instant or guaranteed way to raise a score.

How to Read Your Credit Report

  • Accounts: Check loans and credit cards, current balance, account status and payment history.
  • Enquiries: Check whether recent loan/card enquiries were actually made by you.
  • Personal details: Verify name, date of birth and other identifying information.
  • Payment history: Look for late-payment or overdue entries that may be incorrect.

What If You Find an Error?

Do not ignore an incorrect credit-report entry. Contact the relevant lender and/or credit information company to request correction or updating of the information.

RBI's financial-awareness guidance says individuals have access to one free full credit report including the credit score once each calendar year. It also describes a complaint route through the RBI Ombudsman if an inaccuracy complaint remains unresolved for more than 30 days or the borrower is not satisfied with the resolution.

💡 Credit Score Improvement — A Practical 90-Day Plan

Improving a credit profile is usually a gradual process. The most useful approach is to focus on consistent repayment, controlled credit usage and accurate credit-report information.

Days 1–30: Check and organize

  • Check your latest credit report and identify all active loans and credit cards.
  • Make a list of outstanding balances, minimum payments and due dates.
  • Look for incorrect overdue amounts, account status or unfamiliar enquiries.
  • Set reminders or automatic payment arrangements for upcoming dues.

Days 31–60: Control credit usage

  • Keep credit-card balances manageable relative to the available limit.
  • Avoid unnecessary new loan or credit-card applications.
  • Focus on reducing outstanding high-cost credit where possible.
  • Avoid missing even small required payments.

Days 61–90: Maintain consistency

  • Continue making all required payments on time.
  • Review whether outstanding balances are gradually reducing.
  • Check your credit report periodically for changes or errors.
  • Continue good credit behaviour rather than looking for a quick score-fixing solution.

Does paying off a loan immediately guarantee a higher score?

Not necessarily. Credit scores are based on information in your credit history, and lenders may consider several factors. Closing or repaying an account can change your credit profile, but there is no guaranteed score increase from a single action.

What should you avoid?

  • Missing EMI or credit-card payments.
  • Repeatedly using a very large portion of available credit.
  • Applying for multiple loans or cards without a clear need.
  • Ignoring errors in your credit report.
  • Paying anyone who promises a guaranteed or instant credit-score improvement.

Important: There is no guaranteed 30-day or 90-day method to achieve a particular credit score. Credit improvement depends on your credit history, reported information and continued financial behaviour.
