Calculation Methodology

This page explains how to read estimates from Smart Money calculators, what assumptions may affect the result, and what our tools cannot tell you. Our calculators are intended for general financial education and planning in India.

1. What a calculator result means

A calculator applies a mathematical model to the information you enter. The result is an estimate for that scenario, not a quote, approval, promise, forecast or guaranteed outcome. If an input is missing or inaccurate, the result may not reflect your actual situation.

2. Inputs and assumptions

Each calculator uses the inputs shown on its page, such as amount, rate, duration, contribution, income or expenses. Some calculations require simplifying assumptions—for example, a constant interest rate, regular contributions, a fixed payment schedule or monthly compounding. Where relevant, the calculator page should explain its assumptions and limitations near the result.

Actual products may use different calculation conventions, payment dates, compounding rules, fees, taxes, eligibility criteria or contractual terms. Always compare the calculator assumptions with the terms of the bank, lender, government scheme or financial product you are considering.

3. Rates, rules and information sources

Rates and financial rules can change. For time-sensitive information, prefer primary sources such as government departments, regulators, official scheme operators and banks or financial institutions that provide the relevant product. Secondary sources may be useful for explanation, but should not override an applicable official notification or product document.

When a rate is editable, check the latest applicable rate before relying on an estimate. A rate displayed as a default is an input for illustration and may not be the rate available to you.

4. Rounding and displayed values

Results may be rounded for readability, commonly to whole rupees or a limited number of decimal places. Calculations may use more precision internally than is displayed. Because of rounding and payment schedules, a displayed total may differ slightly from a lender's statement or an institution's final calculation.

5. Testing and corrections

We aim to make the tools understandable and to review calculation logic, input validation and edge cases. A calculator may still contain errors or behave differently from a specific provider's system. If you find a result that appears incorrect, please contact us with the calculator name, the inputs used and the result you expected. Do not include account numbers, passwords, card details or other sensitive personal information.

6. Investment and market-risk estimates

Investment calculators that use an assumed return show mathematical scenarios only. Market-linked investments do not deliver a fixed or steady return; actual values can be lower or higher, and you may lose money. Past performance does not guarantee future results. Taxes, inflation, fees and product-specific rules may not be included unless the calculator explicitly says so.

7. Independence and advertising

Smart Money is an independent financial education and calculator project. Advertising, if displayed, does not determine calculator formulas or the numerical results generated from your inputs.

8. Not financial, tax or legal advice

Important: Smart Money provides general educational information and estimates only. It does not provide personalized financial, investment, tax or legal advice. Before making an important decision, verify the relevant terms with the provider and consider speaking with a qualified professional.
