50/30/20 Budget Rule in India

A simple starting point for managing your salary is to divide your take-home income into three broad buckets: needs, wants, and savings or debt repayment. The 50/30/20 rule can make monthly money planning easier—but it should be adapted to your real expenses.

In one line: Aim for about 50% of take-home income for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and extra debt repayment. These are guidelines, not strict rules.

What does the 50/30/20 rule mean?

  • 50% — Needs: Essential rent or home costs, basic groceries, utilities, necessary transport, essential insurance and minimum required loan or credit-card payments.
  • 30% — Wants: Dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, non-essential shopping, holidays and lifestyle upgrades.
  • 20% — Savings and extra debt repayment: Emergency savings, long-term investments, goals and additional payments toward expensive debt after required minimums are covered.

Use take-home pay—the amount that actually reaches your bank account—rather than your CTC. Your CTC may include employer contributions, benefits and other components that are not paid to you as monthly cash.

This is an illustration, not a recommendation that everyone should spend these exact amounts. If rent, family responsibilities or essential repayments take more than half your income, adjust the percentages to fit your circumstances.

How to use the rule when expenses are high

  1. Start with the essentials. List fixed costs such as housing, food, utilities, transport and required minimum debt payments.
  2. Separate needs from wants. Some costs, such as a phone plan or transport, may have both essential and optional parts.
  3. Build a small buffer. If you have no emergency savings, begin with an affordable amount and gradually work toward a suitable emergency fund.
  4. Prioritise costly debt. Pay required minimums on all accounts. Where possible, direct extra repayment toward high-interest debt while keeping essential expenses covered.
  5. Review monthly. Compare the plan with actual spending and change the amounts when income or expenses change.
When the 50/30/20 split does not fit: You do not need to force your budget into these percentages. In a high-cost month, a temporary 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 split may be more realistic. Focus first on essentials, required payments and avoiding new expensive borrowing.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use gross salary or in-hand salary?

Use take-home salary for the basic 50/30/20 calculation because it is the amount available to spend. If income varies, use a conservative average and budget around essential costs first.

Does the 20% category include debt repayment?

It can include savings and extra debt repayment. Required minimum payments should be treated as obligations in your monthly budget; any additional repayment can be planned in the savings/debt bucket when affordable.

Is the 50/30/20 rule suitable for every family in India?

No single split suits everyone. Housing costs, city, dependants, medical needs, income stability and existing debt all affect what is realistic. Treat the rule as a starting point, not a rigid standard.

What if I cannot save 20% right now?

Start with an amount you can maintain—even a small regular amount—and review spending for changes that do not compromise essential needs. Increase savings gradually when your budget allows.
