Credit Card Minimum Due: What It Means

The minimum amount due is the smallest payment your card issuer asks you to make by the due date under the card's terms. Paying only this amount is not the same as clearing the entire outstanding bill.

Why paying only the minimum can cost more

When you carry an unpaid balance, interest and applicable charges may continue to accumulate. New purchases may also lose the interest-free period under the issuer's terms. The exact rate, fees and payment allocation depend on your card agreement.

Build a practical repayment plan

  1. Check your latest statement for the total balance, minimum due, interest rate and due date.
  2. Where possible, pause new card spending while repaying the existing balance.
  3. Pay at least the required minimum on time to avoid preventable late-payment consequences.
  4. If affordable, pay more than the minimum to reduce the outstanding principal faster.
  5. If you have several balances, list their rates and consider directing extra repayments to the highest-cost balance while meeting minimums on others.
  6. Contact your issuer early if you cannot make a payment and ask what legitimate repayment options are available.

Information needed for a payoff estimate

  • Current outstanding balance
  • Interest rate or APR
  • Monthly payment you can realistically afford
  • Fees, due dates and any new purchases

A payoff estimate is only as accurate as its inputs. Actual charges depend on issuer terms, payment timing and applicable rules.
