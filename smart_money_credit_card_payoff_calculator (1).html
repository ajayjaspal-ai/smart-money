CREDIT CARD PAYOFF GUIDE

💳 Credit Card Payoff Calculator — How Does It Work?

A credit card payoff plan helps you understand how your outstanding balance, interest rate and monthly payment can affect the time and total cost required to become debt-free.

How credit card interest affects your debt

Credit cards can carry relatively high interest rates. When a balance remains unpaid, interest can increase the amount you need to repay. Paying only the minimum amount may take longer to clear the balance.

What affects your payoff time?

  • Outstanding balance: A higher balance generally requires more time or larger payments to repay.
  • Interest rate: A higher interest rate can increase the cost of carrying the balance.
  • Monthly payment: Paying more than the minimum, when affordable, can reduce the balance faster.
  • New purchases: Adding new spending while repaying existing debt can make the payoff period longer.

Why paying more than the minimum can help

The minimum payment keeps the account current according to the card's terms, but it may not reduce the principal quickly. If your budget allows, paying an additional amount can help reduce the outstanding balance faster and may reduce future interest costs.

Example

Suppose you have a ₹1,00,000 credit card balance and can pay ₹10,000 every month. Use the calculator above to compare the estimated payoff time and interest with different monthly payment amounts.

Debt payoff strategies

  • High-interest-first approach: When you have multiple debts, you can direct extra repayment toward the debt with the highest interest rate while maintaining required payments on other debts.
  • Smallest-balance-first approach: You can focus extra repayment on the smallest balance first while maintaining required payments on other debts.

Before using a balance transfer or cash advance

Check the applicable interest rate, transfer or transaction fees, promotional period, taxes and other card-specific terms. A lower promotional rate does not necessarily mean a lower overall cost.

Important things to remember

Try to make required payments on time, avoid unnecessary new spending on a balance you are trying to clear, and regularly review your credit card statements for interest, fees and taxes.

Important: This guide and calculator are for educational and planning purposes. Actual credit card interest, fees, taxes, payment allocation and payoff time depend on the card issuer and the terms applicable to your account.
